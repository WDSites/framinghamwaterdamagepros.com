Who does what after a roof leak

A roof leak has two problems, and they need two different trades. The roof itself belongs to a roofer, who finds the failed flashing, missing shingles or ice dam and closes it up. Everything the water touched inside the house belongs to us. That includes insulation, ceiling drywall, framing, flooring and furniture.

We do not tarp roofs or climb them. We dry the interior so the damage stops growing, and we tell you honestly what can stay and what has to come out. If a roofer cannot come right away, the dry-out still has to start, because wet insulation and drywall keep absorbing water for days.

Why roof leaks hit Framingham homes in winter and spring

Framingham gets freeze and thaw cycles, wet snow and wind driven rain, and each one tests a roof differently. Ice dams form when heat escapes into the attic, melts snow on the upper roof and lets the water refreeze at the cold eaves. The ice then backs water up under shingles and into the walls below.

The Massachusetts building code addresses this problem. Its rule on ice barriers, section R905.1.2 of the residential code, calls for two cemented underlayment layers or a self-adhering membrane. It must run at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line in areas with a history of ice at the eaves. Roofs built before that practice became standard may have no such protection.

Insurers look at whether ice dam damage was sudden and whether the roof was kept up, according to Division of Insurance guidance. That is one more reason to photograph the roofline and the ceiling before repairs begin.

Roof leaks are slow to announce themselves, and the symptom often sits far from the hole.

Safety first when a ceiling is leaking

Keep people out from under a sagging ceiling. If it bulges, place a bucket under the lowest point and do not puncture it unless you are ready for a rush of water. Move furniture and electronics out of the room first.

If water is dripping from a light fixture, switch off that circuit at the panel, but only from dry footing. If you cannot, call the utility or 911 about electrical hazards before you call us. Do not climb onto a wet or icy roof to look for the leak. Photograph the stain from inside and leave the roof to a roofer.

How we dry a roof leak

  1. Map the wet area

    A moisture meter and thermal imaging camera show how far water traveled through the ceiling and down the walls. The wet area is usually larger than the stain.

  2. Open the ceiling where needed

    Sagging or soaked drywall is cut out back to a dry edge, and wet insulation above it is removed so it stops holding water against the framing.

  3. Dry the cavity

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings on joists, rafters and sheathing drop to dry levels. In an attic, crews work with the roofer so repairs and drying do not collide.

  4. Clean and prepare for repair

    Damaged material is bagged out, surfaces are cleaned and the area is ready for water damage repair once the roof is sealed.

Attic insulation, ceilings and the problem of hidden mold

Wet attic insulation looks harmless and keeps working against you. Fiberglass that stays damp packs down, loses its value and holds water against wood. Cellulose can slump and stay wet for weeks.

Moisture that lingers in framing gives mold what it needs to grow, so the dry-out is also the first line of protection. If growth is already visible, the crew sets containment before any cutting. See mold remediation for how that work is handled. We make no promise about what will or will not grow back later, because that depends on the moisture that remains.

Insurance and permits in plain terms

Roof leaks fall into a gray area, so ask your insurer early. Sudden storm damage and ice dam damage can be handled differently from slow leaks caused by age or lack of upkeep. Flood water that enters from the ground is another category entirely. Photograph the ceiling, the attic and any visible roof damage before anything is moved.

For permits on roof or structural work, the Framingham Inspectional Services Division at 508-532-5500 can say whether your project needs one.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to prevent the next leak

A few habits keep a small leak from becoming a rebuild.

Roof leak checklist

  • ✓Do not paint over a stain until the roof is fixed and the area is dry.
  • ✓Do not leave wet insulation in place because the stain looks small.
  • ✓Check the roofline and gutters after a storm from the ground.
  • ✓Keep attic ventilation clear so heat does not melt snow unevenly.
  • ✓Schedule a roofer to inspect flashing at chimneys, dormers and valleys.
