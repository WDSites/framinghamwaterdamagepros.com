Water damage repair starts where drying ends

Water damage repair is the rebuild phase. After water has been stopped, removed and dried, the materials that were cut out or ruined have to be replaced so the room is a room again. That means drywall, insulation, trim, flooring, cabinets, paint and sometimes framing.

This page is about repair after the damage is under control. If water is still spreading, call the 24/7 dispatch line first and start with water mitigation. Repair done over wet material fails, and the second repair costs more than the first.

Most repair calls begin with a stain, a bulge or a floor that has started to cup. A crew inspects, explains what is wrong, explains the scope and how the price is set, and only then starts work.

How a water damage repair job runs

The order matters. Skipping a step is how paint peels and floors lift six months later.

  1. Confirm the structure is dry

    Moisture readings on framing, subfloor and wall bottoms must match a dry reference before anything is closed up. If the readings are high, the repair waits.

  2. Fix or confirm the source

    A leaking roof, a failing pipe or a bad window flashing has to be repaired by the right trade. Repairing the room first only hides the next leak.

  3. Check permit needs

    Cosmetic repair usually does not need a permit, but structural work, electrical and plumbing often do. Permit rules vary, so ask the Framingham Inspectional Services Division at 508-532-5500 or check the online portal.

  4. Rebuild

    Replace insulation, hang and finish drywall, and install trim and flooring. Large patches need taping, mudding and sanding in several coats with drying time between.

  5. Paint and finish

    Prime stained areas with a stain-blocking primer, then paint a full wall to avoid a visible patch.

Ceiling and wall water damage repair

Ceilings tell the story first. A brown ring, a sag or a bulge usually means water is sitting above. Do not puncture a bulging ceiling unless you are ready for a rush of water, and do not paint over a stain until the source is found and the area is dry.

Small, dry stains often need only stain-blocking primer and paint. Sagging or soft drywall is cut out back to a dry, solid edge, the insulation above is checked and replaced if wet, and new board is hung and finished. Ceilings under a bathroom or a roof leak need extra care because the leak may have returned several times.

Walls wick water upward from a wet floor, often a foot or more above the waterline. Crews cut a clean line above the wicking, replace insulation behind it and tape and finish the patch so it blends.

Water-damaged hardwood floors: what to expect

Hardwood is the repair people worry about most. Boards absorb water, swell and cup, meaning the edges rise higher than the middle. Do not sand while the wood is still wet, because boards that look level while swollen can shrink into gaps once they dry.

Cupping often eases if the floor is dried slowly and evenly, and many floors can then be sanded and refinished. Boards that have crowned, cracked or buckled off the subfloor are replaced. The timeline is weeks, not days, because rushing the drying can split boards.

Roof leaks and where repair stops

Callers often describe a ceiling stain after rain, a collapsed section or a leak around a chimney or window. Roof and exterior repairs belong to a roofer, siding or window contractor. We repair the interior damage after the source has been fixed.

In Massachusetts, ice dams add another cause. The Division of Insurance notes that ice dam damage may be covered if it is sudden and not caused by lack of maintenance. Ask your insurer before work begins and keep photos of the stains and the roofline.
