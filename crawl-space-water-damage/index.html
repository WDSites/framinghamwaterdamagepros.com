Crawl spaces here are less common than basements, but they hold water well

Most Framingham houses sit over a full basement, and a crawl space is the lesser case. You find them under additions, in some older homes and in some ranch style houses. A crawl space is a low area between the ground and the first floor, usually with a dirt or thin concrete floor, a few feet of headroom and a hatch to get in.

That design is the problem. Water that enters has no drain, a basement pump may not exist, and nobody looks down there for months. A leak or a spring of groundwater can sit under the floor and feed the wood above it long before the living room shows a sign.

If you have a full basement with water on the floor, the main pages are emergency water removal and flood damage restoration. This page is for the space under the floor.

What standing water does under a house

Floor joists and the subfloor above them are wood, and wood takes up moisture from humid air as well as from direct contact. A crawl space with a wet floor can raise the moisture in those framing members and in the flooring they hold up. Floors may cup, squeak, sag or feel soft in one patch.

Fiberglass insulation between the joists falls when it gets wet and then stays wet. A plastic vapor barrier on the ground can hold a pool of water on top of it. Ductwork, wiring and plumbing also run through the space, so a flooded crawl space can touch every system in the house.

A musty smell inside the rooms above, a warm and sticky feeling, and condensation on pipes below are all clues that the space under you is wet.

Crawl space safety: stay out of standing water and watch the wiring

Do not crawl into a space with standing water. The area is cramped and dark, and wiring, a sump pump or a light fixture may be near the water. Shut off power to the circuit if you can do it from a dry spot, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical doubt.

If water came from a sewer line, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away. Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is the source, then call us.

How a wet crawl space is dried

Working in a tight space slows everything down, so crews plan the setup before anyone goes in.

  1. Get in safely and find the source

    The crew checks the hatch, the lighting and the wiring first, then looks for the source. It might be a pipe above, a failed pump, a foundation seep or surface water coming from outside.

  2. Pump out the standing water

    Submersible pumps and a hose move the water outside, away from the foundation. Small extraction units lift what the pump cannot reach, since a pump leaves a film behind.

  3. Remove what cannot dry

    Soaked insulation and torn or pooled vapor barrier are cut out and bagged. Wood framing is left in place and dried, not removed, unless it has softened.

  4. Dry the space and the floor above

    Air movers push dry air along the joists. LGR dehumidifiers lower humidity in the space, and ducting runs through the hatch. Technicians read moisture in joists and subfloor each visit.

  5. Verify and hand off

    The drying log shows the readings. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference standard for structural drying, and it calls for measurement before equipment comes out. Insulation and vapor barrier are replaced through repair if needed.

Where the water comes from in this area

Framingham sits among rivers, brooks and ponds, and groundwater is a regular visitor. The city's 2023 hazard mitigation plan update, approved by FEMA, notes that many cellars flood because of groundwater, and that the Fire Department lends sump pumps to homeowners. A crawl space has the same exposure with less protection.

Spring thaw and heavy rain raise the water table, and gutters that dump water next to the foundation add to it. A plumbing leak above the space is another common cause, especially from a supply line or a drain under a bathroom or kitchen.

Plumbing repairs in Massachusetts generally need a permit issued to a licensed plumber, under the state plumbing regulation. If the structure itself needs rebuilding, the Inspectional Services Division at 508-532-5500 can tell you whether a permit is needed.

Insurance in general terms

Whether a crawl space loss is covered depends on the source. Sudden and accidental water from a burst pipe is often covered. Groundwater, surface flooding and gradual seepage are often not, and flood damage is generally excluded from standard homeowners policies, as the Massachusetts Division of Insurance states.

The Division also suggests checking your declarations page for a water backup or sump pump failure endorsement. Take photos before anything is removed, keep receipts for emergency steps and call your insurer early.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not close the hatch and hope it dries. A wet space with no airflow keeps its humidity. Do not run a household fan across a wet dirt floor for days and call it done, because it moves the moisture around without removing it.

Do not stuff new insulation in over wet wood, and do not point a pump discharge at the foundation wall. Water that comes back in a day is a fix that did not work. Fix the source, dry the space, then rebuild.
