What makes carpet different from other flooring

Carpet is a stack of layers, not one material. The visible carpet sits on a cushion called pad, which is held at the edges by tack strips, and all of it sits on a subfloor of plywood or a concrete slab. Water can sit in any layer, and each layer dries at its own speed.

That is why wet carpet is a bigger job than it feels. The surface can seem damp for a day while the pad holds water and the floor beneath stays wet. Fast action decides whether the carpet is dried or thrown away.

The city's housing market analysis counts about 12,500 rental units in Framingham next to about 15,300 owner-occupied homes. Carpet is common in rentals and in finished basements, so a single leak can put a lot of tenants, landlords and finished space at risk.

Clean, gray or black water: what happens to the carpet

The source and the age of the water decide the plan. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference standard that sorts water into categories.

Clean (Category 1)Broken supply line, tub overflowUsually dried in place if the crew starts quicklyOften replaced even when the carpet is saved
Gray (Category 2)Dishwasher or washer discharge, or a clean leak that sat for daysSometimes cleaned and dried, sometimes removedRemoved
Black (Category 3)Sewage, river or brook flood waterRemoved and disposed ofRemoved

Category is decided on site. Clean water can move to gray after a day or two if it sits.

What you see and what it means

Wet carpet announces itself in a few ways.

  • A squishy feel underfoot. The pad is saturated.
  • Dark rings or tide lines at the carpet edge. Water has wicked upward from the floor.
  • A sour or musty smell after a leak. The pad or subfloor is staying damp.
  • Ripples or humps in stretched carpet. The backing has swelled or lost its grip on the tack strips.
  • Baseboards that swell or stain. Water has reached the wall bottoms.

First-hour safety steps for wet carpet

Stop the water at its source, using the stop valve or the main shutoff. Stay out of the wet area if water has reached outlets, lamps or the panel, and call the utility or 911 before you call us. Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet, because of the shock risk.

Move furniture off the carpet, or put foil or plastic under the legs so wood and metal do not stain it. Pull up what you can, and keep children and pets away from wet areas.

Photograph the carpet and the source before anything moves, then call the dispatch line.

How wet carpet is extracted and dried

The goal is to pull the water out of every layer, not just the top.

  1. Extract

    Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing water from the carpet and pad. More passes remove more water than any amount of drying afterward.

  2. Lift and float

    The carpet is detached from tack strips along one edge and lifted so the pad can be removed and air can move beneath it. This is called floating the carpet.

  3. Read the subfloor

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera check the plywood or slab and the bottom of the walls.

  4. Dry

    Air movers aim under the carpet and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room until the readings match a dry reference.

  5. Clean and reset

    Saved carpet is cleaned, new pad goes in, and the carpet is restretched onto the tack strips.

Pad, tack strips and the subfloor beneath

Pad is spongy, and it holds water far longer than the carpet above it. Even when clean water is dried out of the carpet, the old pad is often replaced because it is inexpensive and slow to dry. Tack strips can be wet, so they are checked and replaced if they have lifted or stained.

Under the carpet, plywood subfloors dry quickly if reached promptly, while a concrete slab needs time, because it holds water and releases it slowly. A slab that is not dry when new flooring goes on can cause lifted edges and odors, so the slab is read before anything is closed back up.

When carpet can be dried in place and when it cannot

Carpet can usually be dried in place when the water was clean, the leak was caught within a day or so and the backing is intact. Wall-to-wall carpet that is detached, floated and dried with air movers often returns to use.

Carpet that sat in sewage or flood water is removed, because it cannot be made safe to live on. Carpet that stayed wet for several days is also removed in most cases because of odor and growth. If a crew tells you to remove carpet on day one, ask for the readings behind it.

Odors after wet carpet

A sour smell after wet carpet means the pad, the backing or the subfloor is still holding moisture. Perfume sprays do not solve it. The fix is to find the damp layer and dry it, then clean the carpet and apply a deodorizer.

If the smell returns after cleaning, the cause is moisture, and a meter reading will find it. If the smell comes with visible growth, mention it when you call so the crew can plan containment before the work starts.

Framingham flooding and finished carpeted space

Basements are where much of the city's carpet is. Framingham's own hazard plan lists flooding along the Sudbury River and Sucker Brook, and a federal grant paid for a flood study of the Walnut Street area. Brook and river water carries soil and runoff, so carpet it reaches is usually removed.

If your house sits near a brook or low ground, check the address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer what is covered. Flood is separate from plumbing leaks and is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies.

Insurance, in general terms

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance describes sudden and accidental water damage as the kind homeowners policies commonly address, with conditions. A burst line soaking a carpet is that kind of loss. A leak that went on for weeks is often handled differently.

Keep samples, photographs and the dates. Ask your insurer how it treats carpet and pad before work starts. Renters should tell the landlord at once and ask about the building's policy.

Mistakes to avoid with wet carpet

A few common mistakes turn recoverable carpet into a total loss.

  • Waiting a day to see if it dries. Pad and subfloor stay wet while the surface seems dry.
  • Running a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • Using only fans pointed at the surface.
  • Replacing pad and carpet before the subfloor reads dry.
  • Walking on wet carpet. It pushes water down into the pad and spreads stains.
