Winter: frozen pipes and ice dams

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental pipe bursts when the home was properly heated and occupied, and that ice dam damage may be covered if it is sudden and not caused by poor maintenance. That is a good reason to keep the heat on during a trip and document your gutter and roof upkeep.

A burst pipe in a Wayland wall can run for hours before anyone sees it. If a ceiling bulges or a wall feels cold and wet, do not poke it open yourself. The weight of trapped water can come down at once, so leave that to the crew and read about water mitigation for how cavities are dried.

Spring and the river corridor

The Sudbury River winds through Wayland and neighboring Sudbury, and Lake Cochituate lies along the town's border with Framingham and Natick. Spring thaw and heavy rain raise both, and low lots feel it first through basement seepage.

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, so a wet basement from rising ground water is usually a flood claim, not a plumbing claim. If this is your situation, ask the crew about flood damage restoration and check what separate coverage you hold.

If water is still spreading in Wayland

Four moves that hold true in any season.

1

Close the main

Find the main valve, usually where the line enters the house, and turn it off fully. Then open a low faucet to relieve pressure.

2

Mind the power

If outlets or the panel are damp, stay clear. If the area is dry and you can reach the panel safely, switch off wet circuits.

3

Move valuables

Carry photos, papers and electronics to the highest dry spot. Prop furniture on blocks or foil.

4

Reach the line

Call the 24/7 number with the source, the rooms involved and how long water has been moving.
