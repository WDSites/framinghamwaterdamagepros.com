When water is spreading in Natick

If water is moving across a floor in Natick, call the dispatch line first. It is answered around the clock, so a midnight pipe failure gets the same response as a Tuesday morning leak. The crew comes from Framingham, about five miles west, along the Route 9 corridor.

While you wait, close the main shutoff if a pipe is the source, lift valuables off the wet floor and keep everyone out of the water. Stay away from outlets and the panel if they are wet. Call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas hazard before you call us.

Four ways water gets into Natick homes

Most calls trace back to one of four sources. A supply line or fixture fails inside a wall or under a sink. A washing machine hose or water heater lets go while nobody is home. A sump pump quits during heavy rain. Or sewer or storm water comes back up through a basement floor drain.

The first three are handled by shutting off water and drying the structure, which is water mitigation. The fourth is a different problem, because sewage and storm water carry contamination. Those jobs follow the flood damage restoration approach, with protective gear and removal of porous materials.

If water is still spreading in Natick

These four moves cost nothing and protect the most. Do them in this order if it is safe.

1

Stop the source

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. If the water is rising from a drain, do not use sinks, tubs or the washing machine.

2

Power down only if dry

Switch off power to wet rooms only when you can reach the panel on dry ground. If the panel area is wet, leave it alone and call the utility.

3

Lift what you can

Move rugs, boxes, electronics and upholstery onto dry surfaces. Foil or plastic under furniture legs keeps stains out of wood.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher what the water touched and how long it has been there. Photos taken before anything is moved help later.

Lake Cochituate and the old aqueduct

Lake Cochituate touches Natick, Framingham and Wayland. The Cochituate Aqueduct was built to carry water from this lake toward Boston, and the lake is still the defining water feature on this side of town.

For a homeowner, the practical point is simple. If your property sits low near the lake, look up your address on the FEMA flood map before the next heavy rain and ask your insurer whether you have flood coverage. Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, and a pump failure is covered only if you added the right endorsement.
