Floodwater is not the same as a leak

Floodwater and sewage backup carry contamination, so the crew treats them as Category 3 water. Carpet, pad and drywall that touched it are usually removed, not dried. A clean supply line break starts as Category 1 and is usually dried in place.

The difference also affects insurance. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, and that a water backup or sump pump endorsement may provide some coverage, so check your declarations pages.

Sudbury's flood plain overlay and your rebuild

Sudbury's zoning includes a Flood Plain Overlay District drawn from the FEMA flood insurance rate map for Middlesex County, covering mapped Zone A and Zone AE areas. The rules restrict new building and fill in the mapped floodway.

If your lot is in or near the overlay, ask the town Building Department before you rebuild after a flood. The rules can affect how much you can change, so keep the crew's written scope and ask before work begins. Our flood damage restoration page explains the cleanup side.

Great Meadows and the Sudbury River wetlands

The Sudbury River runs through Great Meadows National Wildlife Refuge in Sudbury. Wetlands slow and store river water, but they also keep groundwater high on lots nearby.

If your property sits close to wetland, expect a damp basement after long rain and keep a working sump pump with a battery backup. Our water mitigation crews dry the structure after a failure, and you can read how in that page.

Two water systems: the town and the district

Sudbury's public water comes from the Sudbury Water District, an independent body created by state legislation in 1934. It is separate from the town and its Department of Public Works. The District office is at 199 Raymond Road, phone 978-443-6602, and its treatment plants are on East Street and Raymond Road.

Use that split when you call. A leak in the street main goes to the District. A break inside your house is yours to shut off, and we handle the cleanup. If you have a private well, tell the dispatcher.
