Water damage in South Framingham buildings

City documents use the name South Framingham for the downtown and the southern side of the city. The city's housing market analysis says there are generally more rental units in the southern half of Framingham, and that census tract 3831.01 in South Framingham has the highest share of homes built before 1980 in the city, at 91 percent. The same analysis puts the rental share of tracts 3831.01 and 3831.02 at 78 percent and 87 percent.

That figure applies to that one tract and not to every building here. What it tells us is practical. Many buildings in this part of the city hold several households, share plumbing and have more than one person responsible for them, and that shapes how a water loss is handled.

Where South Framingham sits

In the first hour

Follow these in order and only if it is safe.

1

Find and stop the source

Close the shutoff for the fixture or unit. If it is a shared line, tell the manager or the owner at once so the building supply can be closed.

2

Stay away from electricity

Do not use the panel, outlets or cords while standing in water or if the area is wet. Call 911 or the utility for electrical or gas concerns.

3

Do not dry sewage yourself

Water from a drain, toilet or sewer line is contaminated. Keep people and pets out and wait for a trained crew.

4

Document every affected unit

Photograph the unit where it started and any unit below or beside it, with dates, before anything is moved.

Drying across more than one unit

Water from an upstairs bathroom or kitchen can run through the floor into the ceiling and walls below. We use moisture meters to map how far it has traveled, including into rooms that look dry. Standing water is extracted first, and air movers and dehumidifiers go in where the readings call for them. We take repeat readings on each visit until the materials are dry.

We explain the scope and the price before work begins. Rebuild work, such as new drywall or flooring, is separate and is covered under water damage repair. Public sources say many Framingham cellars flood from groundwater, according to the city's hazard mitigation plan. That is a citywide statement and not a finding about South Framingham.

Mold and insurance

Damp materials can grow mold if they stay wet, so drying quickly is the best protection. If mold is already present, we offer mold remediation. We make no health claims about mold.

For insurance, keep photos, receipts and a timeline. In general, sudden and accidental damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not and flood is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy. A landlord's policy usually does not cover a tenant's belongings, so renters should ask about renter's coverage. We cannot promise what a claim will pay. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for South Framingham, so check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.
