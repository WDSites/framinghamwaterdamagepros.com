Water damage near the Common

The City of Framingham describes Framingham Centre as a village built around a green, with stately homes from the early 19th century. The Framingham Centre Common Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1990 and includes 28 buildings. The city also names a second historic district here, the Jonathan Maynard district.

Framingham Centre is inside the Framingham service area. Older buildings need a drying plan that fits their materials, and that is what this page explains.

Where Framingham Centre sits

Route 9 runs through the area. The Common sits north of it, west of Edgell Road, and Pleasant, Maple and Main Streets meet at the Minuteman Statue. These landmarks come from the city and the National Register listing. We do not draw an exact boundary, because the city has not published one. Mailing addresses here are generally in 01701, but a ZIP code is a postal route and not a neighborhood limit.

What the public record says about the area

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey of Framingham says the Centre gradually became a wealthier residential district with high-style villas and later suburban development, and that several high-style buildings from the late Colonial to the early Victorian periods remain.

We found no public record of a water event specific to Framingham Centre, so we make no claim about how often water damage happens here. We also do not say that historic homes suffer more damage than others. The point is narrower. Original plaster, old wood framing and aged floors react differently to water than modern drywall and engineered flooring, and that changes how we dry them.

Your first hour

In order, and only where it is safe.

1

Stop the source

Close the main shutoff for a pipe or appliance leak. If water is coming through a roof, move contents away and catch drips.

2

Stay away from electricity

Do not use the panel, outlets or cords while standing in water or if the area is wet. Call 911 or the utility for electrical or gas concerns.

3

Do not dry sewage yourself

Water from a drain or sewer line is contaminated. Keep people and pets out and wait for a trained crew.

4

Photograph finishes first

Take pictures of plaster, trim, floors and ceilings before anything is removed. Original finishes are hard to describe afterward.

Extraction and drying in a historic home

We check the structure with a moisture meter, extract standing water and set air movers and dehumidifiers so that moisture is drawn out steadily. Readings are taken on each visit until the materials are dry. We aim to save original plaster, trim and flooring where it is dry enough to keep, and we explain what has to be removed and why before cutting anything.

If an exterior feature will be altered, ask the city or the historic district commission whether approval is needed first. That is general guidance, and it is the owner's responsibility to check.

Mold and insurance

Damp wood and plaster can grow mold if they stay wet, so drying quickly is the best protection. If mold is already present, we offer mold remediation. We make no health claims about mold.

For your insurer, photograph damage, keep receipts and write down the timeline. In general, sudden and accidental damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not and flood is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy. We cannot promise what a claim will pay. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for Framingham Centre, so check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.
