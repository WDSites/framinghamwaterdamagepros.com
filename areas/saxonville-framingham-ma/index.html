Water damage in a village built beside the Sudbury River

The City of Framingham describes Saxonville as a community in the northern part of the city, with the Sudbury River running around it on three sides. The village grew around its mills, and the mill buildings still sit beside homes, small businesses and studios. That mix matters when water gets in, because a leak in a converted mill building and a leak in a century-old house are different drying jobs.

Saxonville is inside the Framingham service area. This page covers what usually goes wrong with water in a village like this, what to do in the first hour and how a drying job is run.

Where Saxonville sits

Saxonville lies north of Route 9 along the Sudbury River. Landmarks the city and the state historical commission point to include the Central Street dam, the Saxonville Mills complex, the Danforth Street playground and the restored trestle bridge over the river. We do not draw a boundary line for the village, because the city has not published one. If you are not sure whether your address is in Saxonville, tell the dispatcher your street and the nearest cross street.

Mailing addresses in the village generally use ZIP code 01701, but a ZIP code is a postal route and not a neighborhood limit, so we go by the street address.

What is documented about water and housing here

These points come from public sources and we cite them by type so you can check them yourself.

The City of Framingham community page says the Sudbury River surrounds the village on three sides. The city's 2023 hazard mitigation plan says the Central Street Dam impounds Saxonville Pond, and that the Sudbury River portion of the Wild and Scenic river system begins in Saxonville. The same plan says many cellars in Framingham flood because of groundwater. That statement covers the whole city and is not a finding about Saxonville in particular.

The state historical commission survey of Framingham records workers' housing from the 1820s and 1830s still standing in the village, along with cottages and wood-frame row houses built later in the 1800s. We mention this only to explain why we plan the drying of older construction carefully. We do not claim that older homes have more water damage than newer ones.

Your first hour if water is spreading

Do these in order and only if it is safe.

1

Stop the source

Close the main water shutoff if a pipe or fixture is leaking. If water is coming in from outside, do not go into it.

2

Stay clear of electricity

Do not touch the breaker panel, outlets or cords while you are standing in water or if the area is wet. Call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas concern.

3

Do not dry sewage yourself

If the water came from a drain, toilet or flooded cellar, treat it as contaminated. Keep children and pets out and wait for a trained crew.

4

Document before you move things

Photograph the water, the damage and the items affected. Then lift what you can onto dry surfaces.

How we extract and dry a structure

A job starts with finding where the water went, not only where you can see it. We use moisture meters to check walls, floors and ceilings and write down the readings. Standing water is pumped or extracted first. Then air movers and dehumidifiers are set to pull moisture out of the materials, and we return to repeat the readings until the structure is dry. Water mitigation covers this process step by step.

Older plaster walls and original wood floors dry more slowly and can be damaged by drying too hard, so the equipment plan changes. If walls or flooring have to be cut out, that is rebuild work, and it is covered under water damage repair.

Mold and what we promise about it

Damp materials can grow mold if they stay wet, so fast drying is the best prevention. If mold is already present, we offer mold remediation. We do not make health claims about mold, and if someone in the home is concerned about their health, they should ask a doctor.

Documenting damage for your insurer

Take photos and video, keep receipts and write down when the water started and when you first saw it. In general terms, damage that is sudden and accidental is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy. Read your policy and ask your insurer what they need. We cannot promise what a claim will pay.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for Saxonville. If you want to know about flood risk at your address, check it on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer about flood coverage.
