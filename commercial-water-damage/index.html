What we do for a commercial water loss, and what we do not

Commercial work comes down to a short list. We extract standing water, set up drying equipment, dry the structure, remove and clean what the water ruined, document moisture readings and hand off a clear scope for the rebuild. Dispatch is open 24/7, including nights and weekends.

We do not repair sprinkler systems, electrical panels, elevators or plumbing. Those belong to licensed trades, and we work alongside them. We also do not promise a timeline before we have seen the space, because the size of the loss decides it.

This page speaks to the person in charge, whether you own the building, manage it or lease part of it. The same dispatch line also handles residential work through water mitigation.

Safety and first-hour steps for a business

Get people out of the wet area and keep them out until the water and the power are dealt with. Shut off the water at the nearest valve, or ask the building manager to do it. Stay away from wet outlets, panels and equipment, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk.

If water is coming from a sprinkler or a fire line, call a fire protection contractor, because we do not work on those systems. Then call us.

Fire protection systems and the city

Framingham's Fire Department regulates work on fire protection systems. The city's Fire Prevention page says that work which shuts down, disconnects or obstructs existing fire equipment needs a separate Fire Department permit, issued after a building permit and an acceptable impairment plan. The Fire Prevention Office is reachable at 508-532-5930.

That matters to a business owner because a wet building sometimes involves a sprinkler line. If a sprinkler head has failed or a line must be shut, let the contractor and the Fire Department handle the system. We dry the building.

How a commercial job runs

The phases are the same as in a home, but the scale and the need to keep a business moving change the plan.

  1. Assess and document

    The crew walks the space with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera and records which areas are wet. You get a written scope and an explanation of how the price is set before work starts.

  2. Extract the water

    Truck-mounted and portable extraction units and submersible pumps remove standing water. Wet carpet tile, pad and loose materials are lifted where they cannot dry.

  3. Protect operations where possible

    Work can be phased so part of a building stays usable. Equipment is placed to leave aisles and exits clear, and drying zones are set up room by room. What can be done depends on the layout and the damage.

  4. Dry the structure

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry floors, wall bottoms and ceilings. Ceiling tiles and wet insulation come out, and concrete floors are monitored until readings drop.

  5. Clean, log and hand off

    Surfaces are cleaned and wet materials are removed. A drying log records the readings, and the repair scope is handed to the owner or the builder for the rebuild.

Large spaces, big equipment, and what drying means

Commercial spaces are open, and open floors take a lot of equipment. A big floor needs many air movers and dehumidifiers to move enough air and remove enough moisture, and the power for them has to come from circuits that can carry the load. Your building manager can help identify those circuits.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference standard for professional water damage restoration, and it is built on measuring moisture rather than guessing. Ask any contractor what readings they will show you and how often.

Drying has no fixed schedule. Slabs, plaster and stacked inventory release moisture at different speeds, so the job ends when readings reach the dry standard.

Landlord and tenant: who handles what

Who is responsible depends on the lease. In general terms, the owner is often responsible for the building, including its structure and systems, and a tenant is often responsible for its own contents and improvements. Insurance on each side usually follows that split.

Do not assume. Read the lease, notify the property manager in writing and ask who will call the plumber, who will call a restoration crew and who will deal with the insurer. Agree on that before any work starts so no one is surprised by a bill.

Records, inventory and insurance

Commercial policies differ widely, so read yours or call your agent early. Some policies include business interruption or loss of income coverage, and some do not. Do not assume either way. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises photographing or filming damage before temporary repairs and keeping receipts for those repairs.

It also advises calling your agent or claims line as soon as you can, since a policy may set a deadline for notice. Keep damaged property for the adjuster to see when you are able. A written list of damaged inventory and equipment helps the claim move.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered and gradual leaks often are not. Flood damage is generally excluded and needs its own policy, so find out which kind of loss you have.

Local factors for Framingham buildings

The 2023 hazard mitigation plan update, approved by FEMA, records that many cellars in Framingham flood because of groundwater. A basement or a lower level used for storage or utilities carries that exposure, and it is a good reason to know where your shut-offs and floor drains are before an emergency.

Permits can come into play for plumbing and structural repairs. Plumbing work generally needs a permit issued to a licensed plumber, under 248 CMR, and the Inspectional Services Division at 508-532-5500 can say what a rebuild needs.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not let staff or customers walk through wet areas to keep working. Do not run extension cords across standing water, and do not move stock back until readings are dry. Do not throw away damaged items before photographing them and listing them.

And do not delay the call to the owner or the insurer while you tidy up. Early notice protects your options and shortens the damage.
