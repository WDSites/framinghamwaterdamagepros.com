Local knowledge

Water damage across Framingham, place by place

Framingham is a long city, and where you live changes the likely cause. Water comes from pipes inside the house and from the outside in, and the landscape has a say in both.

Along the Sudbury River and Sucker Brook

Saxonville, the historic mill village on the river, and the neighborhoods around Walnut Street sit near the two waterways the city's hazard planning names as flood sources. If your home is near either, check the FEMA flood map and confirm whether you carry flood insurance, since standard homeowners policies do not cover flood.

Around Farm Pond, Learned Pond and Lake Cochituate

Basements close to ponds and lakes sit near wetter ground. If you live nearby, test your sump pump and its backup before storm season, and look on your declarations page for a sump pump failure or water backup endorsement, which the Massachusetts Division of Insurance suggests checking.

Framingham Centre, Nobscot and Pinefield

In any older house, including those in historic Framingham Centre, pipes in exterior walls and unheated spaces are where a winter freeze tends to burst a line. Insulate exposed runs and open cabinet doors under sinks during a cold snap.

The Golden Triangle and the Route 9 and I-90 corridors

Commercial buildings near the Natick line and along Route 9 deal with roof drains, sprinkler lines and tenant-space leaks. A business hit after hours needs a crew that answers at night, and so does the property manager who has to document the loss.

Around Framingham State University and MetroWest Medical Center

Large buildings and rentals nearby mean tenants and landlords both call about leaks. Tenants who are unsure who to call can ask the city Inspectional Services Division, which handles code enforcement.

Insurance claims

Working alongside your insurer

Documentation for your claim

We photograph damage, log moisture readings and write down the scope of work so you have clear records. In general terms, sudden and accidental damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood is usually excluded. Check your policy and ask your insurer what they require.
