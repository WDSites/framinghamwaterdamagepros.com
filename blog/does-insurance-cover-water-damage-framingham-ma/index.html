Does Insurance Cover Water Damage in Framingham, MA?

The answer depends on where the water came from. Massachusetts Division of Insurance guidance draws a clear line between sudden household water damage and flood damage.

By the Framingham Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026-10-29.

(508) 446-9244All services

It depends on the source of the water

Whether insurance covers water damage depends mostly on the cause. Per the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, damage from a non-flood source, such as a burst pipe, falls under a standard homeowners policy, which usually covers damage that is sudden and accidental. Flood damage is another matter: the Division states that standard home insurance policies do not cover flood damage.

That single distinction explains most coverage surprises. A pipe that burst in the wall and a creek that rose into the basement can leave the same wet carpet, yet they sit in different insurance categories.

Your own policy has the final word. Read the water damage section, check the declarations page and ask your insurer or agent about anything unclear. Nothing here is a promise of coverage.

Sewer backup and sump pump failure

Sewer and drain backups are a common surprise. The Division of Insurance explains that flood policies cover water that backs up through sewers or drains, sump pump discharge or seepage only when a flood in the area is the proximate cause.

A backup caused by a clogged line, with no flood, is not a flood policy matter. The Division's advice is to check your declarations page for a sump pump failure or water backup endorsement, which may provide some coverage. Many homeowners learn they lack it only after a loss, so look now.

If you are unsure whether your sump pump has coverage, call your agent and ask about the limit as well, since endorsements often carry smaller limits than the main policy.

Flood insurance is separate

Flood insurance is available through the National Flood Insurance Program and from private insurers. The Division lists the NFIP number as 877-336-2627 and publishes a list of licensed private flood insurers on its website.

Do not assume you need it only if you live next to water. The Division notes that standard policies exclude flood damage even away from oceans and rivers. Check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and talk with your agent about whether a flood policy makes sense. What to do while you wait for answers

Coverage questions can take time, and water damage does not wait. Photograph the damage, report the loss and begin water mitigation so the harm does not grow. The Division advises protecting the property from further damage where reasonably possible, and insurers generally expect homeowners to take sensible steps.

Keep every receipt for emergency work and keep damaged materials or photographs of them until the adjuster has seen them. If your insurer asks for documentation, a restoration company can provide moisture readings and a written scope.

Ask your insurer whether it wants to inspect before work starts, and whether it has a preferred way to receive photos. Write down the date of every call. If the claim is large or disputed, a short written timeline of what happened and when often helps everyone stay on the same page, and it costs nothing but ten minutes of your time.

Common mistakes that cause coverage surprises

Most coverage disputes grow out of a handful of predictable mistakes. People assume flood damage is included, never read the sublimits for appliance leaks or sewer backup, and wait weeks before reporting. Some throw away damaged materials before anyone has seen them.

Another mistake is letting a contractor answer the coverage question. A restoration company can describe what it sees and provide readings, but only your insurer decides what the policy pays. If anyone promises approval, treat that as a warning sign.

A better habit is to read the water section of your policy once a year, confirm whether you carry an endorsement for backup or sump failure, and store a copy of the declarations page where you can find it in a hurry.

Massachusetts resources for Framingham homeowners

The source type for this section is the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, the state regulator. Its Consumer Services Unit answers coverage questions at 617-521-7794 and takes complaints, and its flood pages explain how standard and flood policies differ.

The Division also lists a licensed private flood insurer directory, and FEMA operates the NFIP. If you live just outside the city, the same state resources apply in Natick and Sudbury, since insurance rules in Massachusetts are set at the state level.

Local permits for rebuild work are a separate question, handled by each town's building office. In Framingham that is Inspectional Services at 508-532-5500.
