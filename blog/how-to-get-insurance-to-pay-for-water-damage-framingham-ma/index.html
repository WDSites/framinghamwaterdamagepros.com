How to Get Insurance to Pay for Water Damage in Framingham, MA

No guide can promise a claim will be paid, but the way you document and report a loss shapes how smoothly it goes. These are the steps Massachusetts guidance points to.

By the Framingham Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026-10-29.

(508) 446-9244All services

The short answer: document first, report early, protect the home

Nobody can make an insurer pay, and no restoration company can promise approval. What a homeowner can control is the quality of the record. Photograph the damage before anything is moved, report the loss to your insurer or agent promptly, and take reasonable steps to stop further damage.

Those three habits line up with guidance from the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, which tells people to photograph the outside and inside of the property, including damaged items and the height of the water, and to protect the property from further damage where reasonably possible. The rest of this guide turns that advice into a practical order of operations.

A practical order of operations after water damage

  1. Make it safe

    Shut off the water source if you can do it safely. If water has reached outlets or the panel, stay out of the wet area and call the utility or 911 about electrical risk first.

  2. Photograph and video everything

    Capture wide shots of each room, then close shots of damaged floors, walls, ceilings and belongings. Include a tape measure or a visible water line when you can. Take more images than you think you need.

  3. Call your insurer or agent

    Report the loss, ask what your policy covers for this type of damage and ask what the insurer wants before work begins. Write down the date, the name of the person you spoke with and any claim number.

  4. Start mitigation

    Drying cannot wait for a decision. Quick water mitigation limits the damage that grows each day, and insurers generally expect homeowners to take reasonable steps to prevent further loss.

  5. Separate and list damaged items

    Keep damaged belongings apart from undamaged ones until the adjuster has seen them, or photograph them before disposal. A home inventory, if you have one, makes the list faster.

  6. Keep every record

    Save the moisture readings, equipment logs, receipts for emergency costs and written scopes of work. A single folder, paper or digital, saves hours later.

Why the cause of the water changes the conversation

The Division of Insurance explains that damage from a non-flood source, such as a burst pipe, falls under a standard homeowners policy, which usually covers damage that is sudden and accidental. The insurer will investigate the cause. Flood damage works differently, and standard policies do not cover it.

That difference is why the first call to your insurer should describe the source plainly. A pipe that burst, an appliance line that failed and a creek that rose are three different conversations. Gradual leaks that went unnoticed for a long time are often treated differently from sudden ones, so note when you first saw the damage and what you did next.

If you are unsure what your policy says, ask your insurer or agent directly. The Division of Insurance Consumer Services Unit also answers coverage questions at 617-521-7794.

Working with a restoration company and your adjuster

A restoration company can document the damage, take moisture readings and provide a written scope that you can pass to your insurer. It cannot decide your claim, and you should be wary of anyone who promises approval or says insurance will definitely cover everything.

Ask for the scope of work and the drying logs in writing, and send copies to your adjuster. If the adjuster wants to see damage before materials are removed, say so on the first call so the crew can photograph and set aside what is needed. Our crews handle water damage cleanup and drying with this kind of paperwork in mind.

Keep your own copy of every document. If your insurer and the contractor disagree on scope, the written record is what settles the question.

Be cautious about signing anything that assigns your claim to a contractor or gives up your right to deal with your insurer. Read every form before you sign it, ask what it does, and take your time. A reputable company will not pressure you to sign on the spot, and you can always call your insurer or agent with questions first.

What to have ready when you call your insurer

Pre-call checklist

  • ✓Your policy number and the date you first noticed the damage.
  • ✓A short description of the source: pipe, appliance, roof, storm or flooding.
  • ✓The rooms affected and whether the damage is still spreading.
  • ✓Photos and video taken so far.
  • ✓A list of damaged belongings with rough age and value.
  • ✓The name and number of the restoration company on site.

Framingham specifics worth knowing

This section uses two source types: Massachusetts Division of Insurance guidance and the City of Framingham's Inspectional Services page. The Division advises documenting damage inside and outside the property and using a home inventory when working with the adjuster.

For rebuild work, building permits in Framingham run through the Inspectional Services Division at 150 Concord Street, with applications handled through the city's online portal. Permit rules vary by the type of repair, so ask Inspectional Services at 508-532-5500 before closing walls or replacing major systems. Skipping a required permit can raise questions at sale time or on a later claim.

If you live just outside the city, the same documentation habits apply in places like Ashland and Sudbury, though each town has its own permitting office.
