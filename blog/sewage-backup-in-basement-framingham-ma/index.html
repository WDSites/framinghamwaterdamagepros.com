Sewage Backup in a Basement: What to Do First in Framingham, MA

Raw sewage in a basement is an emergency. Stay out, protect yourself and call the right people in the right order.

By the Framingham Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026-10-29.

(508) 446-9244All services

What to do in the first ten minutes

If sewage is coming up a floor drain, toilet or tub in your basement, stop using water in the house. Do not flush, run the washer or run the dishwasher, because everything you send down adds to the backup. Keep people and pets out of the basement.

Before you do anything else, check for electrical risk. If water is near outlets, a panel or an extension cord, do not step into it. If the panel is in the wet area, call the utility or 911 and let them handle power. A restoration crew cannot start in a space with an active electrical hazard.

Then make the calls in this order: utility or 911 if there is any electrical or gas concern, your 24/7 restoration line, and your insurer. Take photographs from the stairs before anything is touched.

Why sewage is handled differently from other water

The restoration standard, ANSI/IICRC S500, sorts water into three categories. Sewage is Category 3, often called black water, because it carries bacteria and other contaminants. Water from a flooded creek or river is treated the same way.

This changes the work. Crews wear protective gear, they contain the area so contamination does not spread to clean rooms, and porous materials such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation that touched the water are usually removed and discarded. Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized and then dried.

That is why a sewage cleanup is not a do-it-yourself weekend. Mops and a wet vacuum spread contamination, and carpet that looks salvageable often cannot be made safe.

Do and do not, until a crew arrives

A few small choices in the first hour change how much has to be removed.

Sewage backup: safe first steps

  • ✓Do stay out of standing water and wear waterproof boots and gloves if you must enter a dry stairway.
  • ✓Do open windows in the basement only if it is dry and safe to reach, to improve airflow.
  • ✓Do photograph the water line, the drain or fixture it came from and every damaged item.
  • ✓Do not use a household wet vacuum or a shop vacuum on sewage water.
  • ✓Do not mix cleaning chemicals to treat the area, because some combinations give off dangerous fumes.
  • ✓Do not walk through the water and then through clean rooms. Take off shoes at the stairs.
  • ✓Do not keep running water in the house while the drain is backed up.

How a professional sewage cleanup unfolds

The sequence looks like other water jobs, with more protection and more removal built in.

  1. Contain and protect

    The crew sets up barriers and wears protective equipment so the contamination stays in the affected area.

  2. Extract the water

    Pumps and extraction units remove liquid and solids. The crew notes how high the water reached and which materials it touched.

  3. Remove porous materials

    Carpet, pad, drywall below the water line and soaked insulation are removed in sealed bags. Photographs record this for your insurance file.

  4. Clean and sanitize

    Remaining hard surfaces are cleaned, treated with an antimicrobial product and rinsed according to the plan for that surface.

  5. Dry and verify

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and moisture readings confirm that framing and slab are ready for rebuilding.

The crew decides on site, but this table shows the general pattern after Category 3 water.

Who to call about the cause

A backup has two possible locations: your private line from the house to the street, or the city main. If your neighbors have the same problem, or the backup happens when heavy rain hits, the city's sewer system may be involved. Call the Framingham Department of Public Works and ask whether a problem has been reported on your street.

If only your house is backing up, a licensed plumber needs to find the blockage or break. Cleanup crews do not clear sewer lines, so expect two separate trades. Do not let cleanup start until the cause is fixed, or the room fills again.

Mitigation of the building comes after. Our flood damage restoration team handles Category 3 water, and the follow-on drying and sanitizing falls under water damage cleanup.

Framingham insurance notes for sewer and drain backup

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. It adds that flood policies, through the National Flood Insurance Program or private insurers, cover water that backs up through sewers or drains only when a flood is the cause.

Backups that happen without a flood are a different question. The Division suggests checking your declarations page for a sump pump failure or water backup endorsement, which may provide some coverage. Many homeowners find out they never added one only after the loss.

That is why the call to your insurer comes early. Ask what applies, what documentation they want and whether they need to see the damage before removal. Photograph the water height and damaged items, separate damaged from undamaged belongings and protect the property from further damage where it is reasonably possible.

After the cleanup: preventing a repeat

Ask the plumber whether a backwater valve makes sense for your home. Keep wipes, grease and paper towels out of drains. If heavy rain triggered the backup, find out whether your street has had earlier reports, and check whether a sump or ejector pump needs servicing.

Homes in Natick and Southborough face their own sewer layouts, and some properties in the region rely on private systems instead of a municipal sewer. Ask your town which applies to your street.
