A musty smell or dark spots after a leak? Start here

Most people find mold after a leak, not before. A musty smell in a finished basement, a dark patch on a ceiling below a bathroom or fuzzy spots in a closet that shares a wall with a leaking pipe are typical signs. The visible growth is a symptom. The wet material behind it is the problem.

Mold remediation is the trade term for finding that moisture, containing the affected area, removing contaminated material and cleaning what can be kept. It is a secondary service to water damage restoration because it often follows a leak, a flood or a slow drip that no one caught in time.

Mold needs moisture, so the leak comes first

Mold needs moisture, organic material to grow on and time. Remove any one of them and growth stops. Cleaning mold without fixing the water source is the most common reason it returns, and no honest contractor can promise it never will.

Common sources include a toilet or sink supply leak, an air conditioner condensate line, a roof or siding leak and flooded basements. Mention the source on the first call. If a leak is still active, a plumber or roofer fixes it and our crews handle the water and drying through water mitigation.

How a mold remediation job is run

The process follows ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for professional mold remediation. You can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

  1. Inspect and find the moisture

    The crew checks readings with a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera to find wet material, not just visible growth.

  2. Contain the area

    Plastic barriers and negative air keep spores from moving into the rest of the house. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run during the work.

  3. Remove contaminated material

    Porous materials such as soaked drywall and insulation are cut out and bagged. Wood framing is cleaned or sanded where it can be saved.

  4. Clean and dry

    Surfaces are HEPA vacuumed and wiped, and the area is dried until readings are normal.

  5. Verify

    The crew checks the finished work. Some situations call for independent post-remediation testing, covered below.

Mold removal and black mold cleanup

Callers often ask about black mold in particular. The color does not tell you what kind it is or how serious the situation is, and only lab testing can name a species. Remediation follows the same containment and removal steps whatever the color.

Small patches on hard, non-porous surfaces such as tile or glass can often be cleaned by a homeowner with household cleaner and gloves. Growth on drywall, ceilings, carpet or insulation, or any patch larger than a few square feet, usually needs professional removal because the material has to be cut out.

Questions about health effects belong with a physician. We do not diagnose or give medical advice.

Mold after water damage

Water damage and mold are linked by time. Materials that stay wet more than a day or two are at risk, which is why water mitigation starts with drying before cleanup. If a flood or leak was not dried, the crew may find growth when walls are opened.

In that case the plan changes. Containment goes up first, wet materials come out in a controlled way and the area is dried and checked before any rebuild through water damage repair.

Testing, inspection and clearance for a home sale or loan

Callers often ask about inspection and testing. An inspection finds moisture and visible growth. Testing, such as air or surface sampling, is done by a lab and is a separate step from removal.

Independent clearance testing is done by a third party after remediation. Buyers, lenders or insurers sometimes ask for it before a home sale or loan closes. We explain what the inspection involves when you call. We make no promises about what a lender, buyer or insurer will accept, because that is their decision.

Rules and Framingham resources

State rules on who may perform mold work vary, so ask any contractor whether your state licenses mold work and what that means for your job. The Framingham Inspectional Services Division handles code and nuisance enforcement, and tenants who are unsure who to call about a rental can start there at 508-532-5500.

If the moisture came from flooding, the city floodplain and the FEMA flood map can help you understand the risk. Check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.
