A frozen pipe is a warning, and the thaw is the emergency

Ice inside a pipe does not usually break it by itself. The pipe splits because trapped water has nowhere to go as the ice grows, and the weak spot is often a few feet away from the frozen plug. The pipe may leak very little while it is frozen. The real flow starts when the ice melts.

That timing is why people are surprised. The house was fine at midnight, the temperature rises by morning, and water begins pouring out of a crack nobody knew was there. Our crews are called to many jobs where the pipe froze on Tuesday and the ceiling came down on Thursday.

This page covers frozen pipes that have not burst yet, pipes that split during a thaw, and the water damage that follows. If water is already running through a floor, call now and read the rest while a crew is arranged.

Where pipes freeze in Framingham houses

Older Framingham houses have long runs of supply pipe in exterior walls, over unheated garages and through crawl spaces and basement rim areas. A kitchen sink on a north wall, a bathroom above a garage and an outdoor hose bib with a hose left attached are the classic trouble spots. Additions built over a slab or a crawl space add more cold surfaces.

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance warns that cold snaps raise the risk of frozen and burst pipes. It advises homeowners to insulate pipes, keep the house heated and open sink cabinet doors so warm air reaches the pipes behind them. Those steps cost little and they work.

Framingham's own hazard mitigation planning, presented to the public in July 2022, lists severe winter storms and nor'easters among the hazards the city studies. A storm that knocks out power for a day can drop an unheated house below freezing faster than owners expect.

Safety steps in the first hour

Find the main shut-off valve and turn it off before you try to thaw anything. If the pipe has split, closing the valve stops the flow the moment the ice melts. Open the faucet nearest the frozen section so melting water has a place to go.

Never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame on a pipe, and never use a space heater near wet floors or standing water. Where water touches an outlet, a cord or the panel, keep out of that room. Phone the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas danger before you dial us.

A licensed plumber repairs the pipe itself. The Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters says only licensed plumbers may do plumbing work with the proper permits. We handle the water, the drying and the damage.

Finding the split after the thaw

Locating the break is part detective work. Pipes often fail along a seam, and the crack can be in a spot nobody can see.

  1. Follow the water

    Start where the water shows, then work backward. A stain on one ceiling can come from a pipe two joists over.

  2. Take readings

    A moisture meter shows how far water has traveled in drywall and framing. A thermal imaging camera shows cold, wet areas that the eye cannot see.

  3. Open only what you must

    Small inspection cuts near the suspected line confirm the source without tearing out a whole wall.

  4. Hand off the pipe

    Once the break is found and the water is off, the plumber makes the repair. Drying can begin the same day.

How we remove the water and dry the structure

A split pipe usually releases clean water, so the first job is speed. Extraction units and submersible pumps pull standing water from floors. Wet carpet pad is lifted or cut back when it cannot be dried in place, and soaked insulation in the affected cavity is removed.

Drying then follows a measured plan. Fans called air movers sweep warm, dry air over the wet surfaces while low-grain dehumidifiers pull the moisture back out of the room. Wall cavities get airflow through small holes or lifted baseboards. A technician logs readings at every visit, and the job ends when each material matches its dry reference.

The measure and adjust method follows ANSI/IICRC S500, a published reference for water damage restoration. Our broader water mitigation page explains the same method in more detail.

Insurance and frozen pipes in general terms

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe only if the home was properly heated and not left unoccupied. Its figures show that in 2024, non-flood water losses and freezing damage made up nearly half of homeowner claims in the state, so adjusters see these losses often.

Gradual leaks and neglect are usually treated differently from a sudden split. Flood damage from outside water is a separate matter and is typically excluded from standard policies. Your policy and your insurer decide, so read the wording, take photos before anything is moved and ask what documents they need.

Mistakes to avoid and how to prevent a repeat

Most frozen pipe damage comes from a handful of avoidable moves.

Frozen pipe checklist

  • ✓Do not thaw a pipe with an open flame or a heat gun left unattended.
  • ✓Do not leave the water on while you wait for ice to melt.
  • ✓Do not ignore a trickle during a cold snap, because it signals ice already forming.
  • ✓Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor spigots before winter.
  • ✓Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls.
  • ✓Leave heat on and let a cabinet door stand open on very cold nights, even when you travel.
