How common is an AC leak in Framingham?

Plainly, air conditioner leaks are not the main cause of water damage here. Basement water, burst pipes and appliance failures come first, and the cooling season runs only from late spring into early fall. If your water problem is a wet basement or a pipe, water mitigation is the page that fits.

AC leaks do happen, and they are almost always condensate. An air conditioner pulls humidity out of the air, and that water has to leave through a drain line. When the line clogs, a pan overflows or a wall unit tilts the wrong way, the water goes into the nearest ceiling, wall or floor.

The city's housing market analysis, built on American Community Survey data, reports that about 77 percent of Framingham homes were built before 1980. Many were designed without central cooling, so air handlers were added later in attics, closets and basements, where a slow drain leak can run unseen.

What you see and what it means

A ceiling stain after rain points to the roof. A stain that appears or grows only when the air conditioner runs points to condensate. These are the other common signs.

  • Drips from the front of a wall unit. The drain hose is blocked or kinked.
  • A brown or rust-colored streak down the wall below a mini-split. Water has been running there for some time.
  • A musty smell from a closet that holds the air handler. Insulation or flooring is damp.
  • Water in the secondary pan, or a float switch that keeps shutting the system off. The main drain is not keeping up.
  • A bulge in a ceiling under an attic unit. Water is pooling above the drywall.

First-hour safety steps for an AC leak

Turn the air conditioner off at the thermostat, then at its breaker or disconnect. Water near an air handler means water near wiring, and running the system keeps adding condensate.

If a ceiling is bulging or sagging, keep people out from under it and do not puncture it unless a crew says it is safe. Do not step on the drywall between attic joists. Do not open an electrical panel on the unit while anything around it is wet.

Call an HVAC technician for the drain line and the unit, and call us for the wet structure. Photograph the stain, the pan and the equipment before anything is cleaned or moved.

How we dry and repair after an AC leak

The leak is short, but the damage can sit in places you cannot see, especially above ceilings.

  1. Confirm the source

    The HVAC technician clears or repairs the drain. We trace the water with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera so we know how far it traveled.

  2. Remove what cannot dry

    Wet insulation above a ceiling and sagging drywall are cut out back to dry, solid edges so they stop holding water.

  3. Dry the structure

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers work on the open areas, and readings are logged until framing and subfloor reach a dry standard.

  4. Repair the finish

    New board is hung, taped and finished, stained areas are sealed with a stain-blocking primer, and a full ceiling plane is painted so no patch shows.

Why summer humidity makes drying harder

AC leaks happen in the humid months, when outdoor air is already damp. That works against drying, because wet materials only give up water to air that is drier than they are.

A crew counters it with LGR dehumidifiers that keep the room dry no matter what the weather does outside. Keeping windows closed and the drying equipment running is what lets wet framing and insulation come down to a safe reading.

Insurance, in general terms

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance draws the line between damage that is sudden and accidental and damage that follows poor upkeep. A drain line that was never serviced may be argued onto the wrong side of that line, while a sudden failure usually is not.

Wording differs by policy. Ask your insurer how it treats condensate leaks and worn equipment, and keep the technician's report, your photos and the date you first noticed the stain.

Mistakes to avoid and how to prevent a repeat

Most AC leaks are preventable with a few small checks.

  • Do not paint over a ceiling stain before the cause is fixed and the area is dry.
  • Do not keep running the system with a clogged drain to get through a hot week.
  • Have the drain line cleared at seasonal service and look at the secondary pan in spring.
  • Ask about a float switch that shuts the system off when the pan fills.
  • Keep mini-split units level and the drain hose straight and unkinked.
