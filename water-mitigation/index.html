When water is spreading in your Framingham home

If water is moving through a floor, down a wall or across a basement right now, treat it as an emergency. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and the call takes about two minutes. While you wait for a crew, there are four things you can safely do.

Shut off the water at the main valve if the source is a pipe or a fixture supply line. Lift rugs, books, electronics and small furniture off wet floors. Keep children and pets out of the wet area. Open cabinet doors under sinks so air reaches the wet cavity.

If water has reached an outlet, a power strip or the electrical panel, stay out of the wet area. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us, because a restoration crew cannot start work until the hazard is handled.

Wet carpet, a damp wall or a spreading ceiling stain? That is a mitigation job

Homeowners rarely search for the word mitigation. They type things like "carpet is soaked after a leak", "wall feels damp and cold" or "ceiling stain getting bigger". Those are all symptoms of the same thing: water has gone somewhere it cannot dry on its own.

Water mitigation is the trade name for the first phase of water damage restoration. It means stopping the source, removing the water and drying the structure fast enough that the damage stops growing. The sooner it starts, the less has to be torn out and rebuilt later through water damage repair.

What happens during a water mitigation visit

Every visit follows the same order, whether the water came from a washing machine hose in Nobscot or a failed sump pump near Farm Pond. The order matters because each step makes the next one possible.

  1. Stop the source

    The crew locates the leak and shuts it off, or confirms you already did. A running supply line keeps adding water faster than any pump removes it.

  2. Map the moisture

    Technicians use moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to find water inside walls, under flooring and above ceilings. A surface that looks dry can still be holding water in the cavity behind it.

  3. Extract standing water

    Submersible pumps and extraction units pull out pooled water. Wet carpet and pad are lifted or cut back when the pad cannot be dried in place.

  4. Set the drying equipment

    Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. Wall cavities and floor assemblies get directed airflow so the framing dries, not just the finish.

  5. Monitor until dry

    The crew returns to read moisture levels and adjust equipment. The job ends when materials reach their dry standard, not when a day count runs out.

Structural drying: how walls, floors and framing actually get dry

Structural drying is the part of mitigation that takes the longest and is the easiest to get wrong. Water soaks into drywall, subfloor, framing and insulation, and each of them gives it back slowly. Pointing a single fan at a wet wall dries the paint and leaves the cavity wet.

A proper setup pairs air movers with LGR dehumidifiers so the air stays dry enough to pull moisture out of materials. Technicians record temperature, humidity and moisture readings each visit in a drying log. If a reading stops falling, the setup changes: more airflow in a cavity, a panel opened, or a section removed.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and it is built around this measure-and-adjust approach. You can ask any company whether its crews work to S500 and what readings they will show you.

Water categories and what mitigation does about each

The source of the water decides how the crew handles it. S500 sorts water into three categories, and the category can get worse the longer water sits.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat mitigation involves
Category 1Broken supply line, tub overflow, ruptured water heaterExtraction and drying. Most materials can be dried in place if the crew acts quickly.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, clean water that sat for daysExtraction, cleaning and antimicrobial treatment. Carpet pad and some porous materials are usually removed.
Category 3Sewage backup, river or brook flood waterProtective gear, removal of porous materials and sanitizing. Treated as a flood damage restoration job.

Category is decided on site by the crew. Ask them to explain which one applies to your loss and why.

Where Framingham water damage usually starts

Framingham gets its drinking water through the city Department of Public Works distribution system, with supply purchased from the Massachusetts Water Resources Authority. That means a burst supply line or a failed water heater starts as clean Category 1 water. It does not stay that way, because water that sits in carpet, pad and drywall for more than a day or two picks up contaminants and starts feeding mold.

Flooding is the other main source. The city's hazard planning names the Sudbury River and Sucker Brook as sources of flooding, and a federal grant funded a study of flooding in the Walnut Street neighborhood. If you live in a low area near a brook or pond, check your address on the FEMA flood map and confirm what your insurance covers before the next heavy rain.

Winter adds frozen and burst pipes. Heating and occupancy matter to insurers, so a pipe that bursts in a house kept warm is treated differently from one in a vacant, unheated house. In an older house in Framingham Centre or Saxonville, a pipe in an exterior wall is a common place to look first.

Dry in place or tear out: the honest trade-offs

Mitigation is not the same as removing everything wet. Drying in place saves money and disruption when materials are clean and the crew moves quickly. Tearing out costs more up front and is the right call when the material cannot be made safe or dry.

Carpet over a clean-water leak can often be dried if the pad is lifted and the subfloor is reached the same day. Drywall that has wicked water more than a foot or two above the floor, insulation that is soaked, and any material touched by sewage or flood water are usually removed. If a crew tells you everything must go on day one, ask them to show you the readings that support it.

Mitigation does not waterproof a basement and it does not rebuild what was removed. It stops the damage and gets the structure ready for repair. If you are weighing a basement waterproofing project, that is a separate trade.
